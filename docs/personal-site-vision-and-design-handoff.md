# Personal Website: Vision, Narrative, Design, and Implementation Handoff

**Status:** Concept brief / creative direction / implementation specification, not an approved final design.  
**Target experience:** jonathanreyes.com.  
**Important repository note:** This PR lives in `panbanda/resume` because it is currently the verified writable personal-site-related repository. Its README describes an older JSON résumé workflow, publishing to `resume.jonathanreyes.com`. Do **not** assume this repository deploys the current `jonathanreyes.com` homepage. Locate the production website source before implementing. This PR makes **no live-site changes**.

## 1. The brief in one sentence

Build an unmistakably personal, immersive editorial website that quickly reveals **what Jonathan believes, why he builds, and what others can accomplish because of his work**, then lets every audience find credible evidence without having to decipher an artistic interface.

This is **not** a new technology portfolio, a résumé-first website, or a product showcase. It is a public expression of a life-sized vision, with verifiable work and human outcomes as evidence.

## 2. The personal vision behind everything

Jonathan sees the world as unfinished, not predetermined. People and organizations can question inherited assumptions, change recurring conditions that cause problems, and become capable of more through curiosity, courage, thoughtful action, and learning. He wants to make progress *human*: more families with stability, more individuals able to develop and exercise capabilities, more small enterprises able to support livelihoods, more opportunities for those who have been excluded, and less preventable suffering.

He identifies with two recurring roles:

- **Builder:** explores and brings ideas into existence across code, systems, art, architecture, media, physical making, leadership, and new organizations. The medium changes; creative inquiry remains.
- **Multiplier:** shares knowledge and tools, creates conditions for independence, and helps others build and achieve beyond his direct involvement.

His method: **see beyond what is → question causes → act responsibly → learn from reality → help others carry it further**. He calls the systems-thinking component **moving upstream**: changing conditions, not only treating downstream consequences. Reflection matters; it must not become a substitute for action.

His values are not interchangeable motivational adjectives: curiosity opens alternatives; courage makes experimentation possible; confidence is earned through learning; hope needs evidence and opportunity; agency needs real means to act; adaptability allows response to change; abundance is created through cooperation and ingenuity; thriving includes beauty, dignity, love, and life worth experiencing.

He accepts that innovation sometimes involves difficult tradeoffs and disruption, including job displacement when broad human benefit is real, but rejects technological novelty, corporate growth, and efficiency as sufficient measures of progress. Reskilling, transition paths, distribution of benefits, and the welfare of affected people matter.

**Family is not the cost of the mission.** Jonathan wants his children to remember a present, involved, loving father and his partner to experience dependable attention and shared life. A public site should honor that without exploiting or unnecessarily exposing private family life.

**North-star articulation (working, subject to voice testing):**

> I build things meant to outgrow me.
>
> I'm drawn to the space between how things are and how they could be. I question what we've learned to accept, build better alternatives, and share what I learn so others can go further.
>
> What matters most isn't where my work ends. It's where someone else's begins.

This is editorial positioning, **not a claim that all past work has demonstrated large-scale social impact**.

## 3. The actual Purple Cow (Seth Godin lens)

The unusual thing is **not** “technical leader with animations” or “visionary founder with an inspirational quote.” It is the productive contradiction: **a technically capable, interdisciplinary builder who measures success by growing other people's independence and capabilities, rather than by making himself indispensable**.

A visitor's retellable takeaway should be:
> “He builds powerful things, but his site is really about what other people can now do because of them. You can see the difference between the work and the capability it gives others.”

Apply four strategic lenses as heuristics, never as endorsements:

1. **Seth Godin / Purple Cow:** Remarkable means worth remarking upon by the *right* audience, not universally flashy. A distinctive idea needs credible proof and a natural way to retell it.
2. **Marty Neumeier / differentiation:** Own a specific tension and visual behavior instead of repeating the well-designed tech portfolio formula. Visual polish alone is not differentiation.
3. **Donald Miller / clarity:** An employer, collaborator, or nontechnical visitor should immediately know who Jonathan is and why the work matters. The visitor must not solve an interface puzzle.
4. **Experiential brand design:** Typography, motion, narrative, code demos, and interaction should all express the *same* idea: one act of creation can open paths for others.

**Avoid:** gimmick intro, particle wallpaper, vague superlatives, empty inspiration, status language, forced video, self-celebratory hero case studies, generic card grids, and unverifiable impact claims.

## 4. Who arrives and what they need

| Visitor | First question | Design answer |
|---|---|---|
| Hiring executive | Who is he? What scope and evidence? | Real name and concise professional context always visible; direct `Experience / Résumé` link; case studies and verifiable outcomes |
| Founder / collaborator | Does he spot opportunities, build, and execute? | Provocative point of view followed quickly by concrete projects and decisions |
| Technical peer | Are his claims real? | Source repositories, technical details, limitations, architecture, measured results |
| Creative collaborator | Does he see across media? | Authentic cross-disciplinary artifacts, original visual language, editorial craft |
| Nontechnical visitor | Why should I care? | Human language and recognizable outcomes before frameworks and technology labels |
| Community / civic partner | Does he care about actual people? | Respectful descriptions of service, verified progress, permission-based stories |
| Recruiter / automated screening | Can I get chronology and structured facts? | Accessible conventional experience view and downloadable résumé generated from structured data |

Do not privilege “visionaries” over operators, maintainers, teachers, caregivers, evaluators, or pragmatic problem-solvers. The unifying ethic is deliberate contribution and human improvement.

## 5. Narrative architecture: intrigue → person → proof → philosophy → invitation

### First 5–10 seconds: a personal, slightly surprising conviction

Working hero:

**I build things meant to outgrow me.**

Subhead:

*I question what we've learned to accept, build what could be better, and share what I learn so others can go further.*

Visible attribution: Jonathan Reyes. A compact descriptor such as “Builder · Creative technologist · Systems thinker” can give professional bearings. Provide two unmistakable paths: **Get to know me** (optional film / story) and **See the work**. Immediate résumé access in nav; no gate.

The more poetic line “The world isn't finished” may appear deeper as a supporting motif; it is less differentiated as the first line.

### Act I: meet a person

Authentic portrait and optional short film. Natural, direct-to-camera language; **not a keynote, company promo, or technical résumé spoken aloud**. Reveal lifelong curiosity, making across media, upstream thinking, multiplication, family commitments, and bias to action. Visitors can read a transcript and skip the film entirely.

### Act II: the turn

“What I've come to care about most isn't only what I can make. It's what other people can do because of it.” Show the shift from object to capability.

### Act III: evidence, not a product launch

Curate 2–3 **representative stories** from the full body of work that best demonstrate empowerment or changed conditions. Candidates based on the current site (validate each claim):
- Company-wide AI enablement: adoption and independent workflows by nontechnical teams
- AI-powered ideation: reducing the friction between someone having an idea and being able to articulate and advance it
- Data explorer: giving business teams direct answers without specialized data gatekeepers
- Developer platform / microservices: making other teams independently productive

These are better **homepage evidence candidates** than treating Higgs and Omen as flagships by default. Do not imply enterprise efficiency gains automatically caused social benefit; describe the actual observed enablement.

### Act IV: range, imagination, and the living notebook

Show software, organizational systems, physical making, creative media, art, experiments, community service, and evolving questions. The range is the point: different mediums, one orientation. Some work may simply be playful, aesthetic, or exploratory; do not force an “impact” claim onto it.

### Act V: human purpose and contact

Concrete stakes: a parent able to afford food, a small business supporting families, a person gaining capability or stability. These can be **aspirations/examples**, not fabricated testimonials. Family should be treated with dignity and restraint. End in openness and invitation, not aggressive hiring language.

## 6. Content hierarchy and IA

Primary nav (ordinary and accessible): **Home · Work · Ideas · About · Experience · Contact**. The unique experience belongs in the *story*, not unfamiliar navigation.

- **Home:** editorial opening, person, selected evidence, concise philosophy, ongoing explorations, human purpose, contact
- **Work:** full archive with selectable lenses and filters; includes professional initiatives, independent/open-source work, physical/creative projects; no artificially fixed flagships
- **Work detail:** a human problem → what was accepted → what changed → what it enables → evidence → technical depth / limitations / source
- **Ideas / Notes:** essays, talks, experiments, research, questions; label work-in-progress vs validated insights
- **About / Philosophy:** longer personal story, “move upstream,” action and multiplier values, optional film/transcript
- **Experience / Résumé:** truthful conventional chronology, scope, education, credentials, links, downloadable PDF and JSON
- **Contact:** plain, direct ways to start a conversation

Higgs and Omen belong primarily in the **open-source archive**, not as obligatory homepage flagships. They are *proof of building in public and sharing tools*. Possible succinct positioning:
- **Higgs:** Apple Silicon / MLX local inference, provider routing, performance introspection and desktop console; access, control, observability
- **Omen:** Rust code analysis of complexity, dependencies, hotspots, technical debt, and change risk to inform developer and agent decisions *before* problems occur

Pull details from the current repositories, not stale text from the current website. **Omen is Rust-based in the repository; older homepage copy says Go.** Never claim compiler-proven safety or zero risk from Omen's heuristics.

## 7. Signature experience: the work versus what it enables

Create an optional interaction named internally **The Ripple Effect / One Becomes Many**.

On a selected work story, switch between:
- **What I built:** the precise, tangible artifact, choices, and technical/creative characteristics
- **What it enables:** the verifiable capability, workflow independence, or human benefit it can create

This is not a cosmetic toggle; it is the site's guiding idea made usable. There must be coherent default content and keyboard / screen-reader support. One view must not imply measured results the other cannot substantiate.

An optional secondary “connections” exploration may map ideas, work, lessons, people enabled, and upstream causes. Never make it the only way to browse.

## 8. Art direction: premium, alive, but not formulaic

**Direction:** “Living Atelier” meets a thoughtful documentary and a scientific observatory. Refined editorial typography, genuine texture, rich light/dark rhythm, restraint, and authored imagery. Avoid default startup visuals.

**Initial tokens for exploration (not final brand approval):**
- warm paper `#F3EFE6`
- deep forest ink `#172421`
- pale sage `#A9C4B1`
- aged copper `#C58D68`
- graphite `#252725`

Typography: editorial expressive serif for *ideas*, restrained highly readable sans-serif for the explanatory prose and navigation, mono only for measurements/technical metadata. Current prototype direction experimented with Instrument Serif, Manrope, and DM Mono; assess licensing, load performance, readability, contrast, and alternatives rather than blindly retaining them.

**Logo:** do not rely on a generic `JR.` or monogram alone. Explore a minimal authored mark expressing one idea branching into several paths, or an unusually refined typographic signature. Test at favicon, navigation, video, and print sizes. Avoid looking like a venture fund, AI platform, or generic creative agency.

**Imagery:** photograph / record Jonathan's actual work, prototypes, studio, talks, artifacts, and consenting collaborators. Avoid synthetic pictures presented as documentary proof. Do not treat children, aid recipients, or communities as brand assets.

**Rhythm:** opening in dark atmospheric ink; moments of warm paper and tangible making; distinctive but sparse project chapters; quieter human conclusion. Layout may shift across chapters without causing disorientation. A conventional navigation and content route always remain.

## 9. Motion: meaning, not decoration

Motion metaphor: **one idea becomes multiple paths**. A tiny point/line can grow, diverge, and reveal connected work. Motion should be deliberate, legible, and causally connected to content.

Candidate moments:
1. Opening: a single line branching slowly into a subtle network behind the hero; the text remains readable. No excessive particle field.
2. Narrative reveal: image / text transitions as visitors discover the person behind the conviction. Never hide crucial content solely behind scroll-triggered JS.
3. “What I built / What it enables”: animate the *shift in perspective* (crossfade/morph of a representation) while preserving orientation.
4. Project details: lightweight diagrams reveal connections, dependency or downstream effects as explanatory aids, not ornament.
5. Exit: bring multiple paths together into an invitation to connect / explore.

**Rules:** no forced intro; no autoplay audio; optional video with controls and transcript; honor `prefers-reduced-motion`; maintain keyboard operability, mobile performance, accessible focus, readable contrast; allow content to remain intelligible with CSS animations and JS disabled. Avoid scroll-jacking. Favor transforms/opacity; test real devices and battery/performance.

## 10. Content data and preservation (non-negotiable)

**Do not erase or silently summarize away existing content.** Before redesign, capture a snapshot of the current homepage and all case-study text, links, media, certifications, chronology, and metadata. Maintain a source map and migration audit.

A suggested separation:

```text
content/
  resume.json             # canonical structured career chronology; conventional résumé export
  case-studies.json       # original detailed initiative write-ups and results
  projects.json           # curated project metadata and links, including Higgs/Omen
  writing/                # essays and notes
  vision.md               # versioned public philosophy / script
  archive/                # original unmodified source content and provenance
```

**Caveat:** This repo already has `resume.json` (a legacy JSON résumé with older chronology and contact information). Preserve it unchanged in the planning PR; audit dates, older personal contact details, current role, links, and permissions before surfacing anything as current. The production homepage also contains extensive later initiatives not represented in the old résumé. They must both survive migration.

Do not publish private contact information just because it exists in a repository. Don't import unsourced performance metrics or testimonials as verified outcomes. Keep technical artifacts and human impact differentiated.

## 11. Content voice and example lines

Tone: thoughtful, personal, precise, optimistic without naïveté, conversational, quietly ambitious; avoid consulting jargon, “visionary leader,” hero worship, or endless “I believe” refrains.

**Possible opening:**
> I've never been very good at accepting that things have to be the way they are.
>
> I question things. I take them apart. I try to understand why they work. And sometimes I build something different.
>
> But what matters to me most isn't simply what I make. It's what someone else can do because of it.

**Human motivation:**
> A larger company is not automatically a better world. A faster system is not necessarily a better human experience. I care about what changes for people.

**Bias toward action:**
> There is a time to reflect and a time to begin. At some point, you have to change something.

**Home and responsibility:**
> I don't want to spend my life making the world better while missing the life happening right in front of me.

**Closing possibility:**
> What matters most isn't where my work ends. It's where someone else's begins.

Treat all copy as a **hypothesis to test out loud**, not immutable slogans. Don't cram every line into one page.

## 12. Success criteria and qualitative testing

Show the opening to (a) technical hiring executive, (b) founder, (c) nontechnical visitor, (d) experienced designer, (e) pragmatic engineer. Ask after 10 seconds:
- Who is Jonathan?
- What drives him?
- What would you expect him to be able to do?

Then after 2 minutes:
- What is different about him?
- Which example made the vision credible?
- Can you find the résumé and original technical details?
- What would you tell a colleague about the experience?

Pass condition: respondents remember the **builder → multiplier** thesis and connect it to a *specific verified example*, not merely a nice animation. Recruiters can reach experience / résumé with minimal friction. Nontechnical visitors grasp why the work matters.

Quantitative signals to observe after launch: pathways to work/experience/contact, film engagement (optional/consented analytics), projects reached, and authentic conversations. **Do not optimize for views at the expense of integrity.**

## 13. Phased implementation plan

**Phase 0: Source-of-truth audit**
- Identify actual production website repository, hosting, build/deployment config
- Inventory original site and verify chronology / metrics / broken links
- Record current-source snapshots; create migration mapping
- Distinguish legacy `resume.json` and modern initiative content

**Phase 1: Content and experience prototype**
- Define page narrative and 10-second opener, test with people
- Build two distinct hero variations and compare comprehension, not just beauty
- Produce authentic photo/video shot list and narration script
- Establish typography, responsive design tokens, semantic page skeleton

**Phase 2: Proof and interaction**
- Build selected work stories, `built/enables` lens and full archive
- Add Higgs/Omen as supporting open-source stories
- Implement structured data and résumé view/export
- Add optional motion, reduced-motion mode, video/transcript

**Phase 3: Validation and launch**
- Mobile / desktop / Safari / Chrome / keyboard and screen reader checks
- Accessibility, performance, SEO, schema and link audits
- Verify archived material is reachable and all measurements sourced
- Collect visitor feedback, refine copy/interaction
- Release only after preview approval; do not automatically publish to the live domain

## 14. Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The first screen states a recognizable, differentiated personal conviction **and** identifies Jonathan
- [ ] An employer can reach résumé/history immediately
- [ ] An ordinary visitor can explain the philosophy without jargon
- [ ] At least one featured story shows the difference between the artifact and the capability enabled
- [ ] Higgs and Omen are supporting open-source evidence, not obligatory homepage flagships
- [ ] The older site content and JSON résumé survive with a checked source map
- [ ] Claims and dates are verifiable, and outdated Go/Rust discrepancies resolved
- [ ] Motion clarifies rather than distracts; reduced-motion and no-JS experiences work
- [ ] The film is optional and accessible with transcript
- [ ] Responsive typography and interaction are premium on mobile as well as desktop
- [ ] Project cases support both human-story and technical-depth readers
- [ ] Contact and collaboration actions are easy to find
- [ ] No production site is overwritten during exploratory design

Keep the remarkable idea ("I build things meant to outgrow me") and prove it quickly through verifiable work and real human stakes. Preserve all legacy and current site information through structured data and archives. First locate and audit the actual website source.
